# Bilingual Resume Guidance

Use this reference when creating or translating a Chinese-English resume.

## Principles

Do not translate word-for-word when it produces awkward phrasing. Preserve the candidate's claims, scope, dates, and evidence, but adapt wording to the target language's resume conventions.

## Layout

For a bilingual resume, choose one of these formats based on the user's goal:

- English-first with Chinese support when applying internationally but needing Chinese readability.
- Chinese-first with English support for domestic applications involving international teams.
- Two separate versions when ATS parsing or page length matters.

Avoid doubling every line in a one-page resume if readability suffers. It is often better to create separate Chinese and English versions.

## Consistency

Keep names of degrees, institutions, employers, projects, and publications consistent across languages. Use official English names where available; otherwise provide a clear translation.
